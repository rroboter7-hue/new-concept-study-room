Frequency reference data (2026-10-29)

This site uses a compact derived table for its own curriculum expressions.
No learner writing, personal progress or browser data was used to make it.

Single words: wordfreq 3.1.1, English large wordlist, by Robyn Speer and
contributors. https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq
Derived word-frequency data: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0.
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/
Original software: Apache 2.0. The data combine multiple genres and sources,
including Google Books Ngrams, OpenSubtitles, Wikipedia and other corpora.
Source attribution and bibliography: https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq#sources-and-supported-languages
SUBTLEX wordlists by Marc Brysbaert et al., freely available at
http://crr.ugent.be/programs-data/subtitle-frequencies
Google Books acknowledgement: https://books.google.com/ngrams

Partial-star fill: round(clamp(Zipf - 2, 0.1, 3), 1 decimal).
Zipf >=5 fills all three stars; unmeasured words get no score.
The compact source table also retains coarse tiers (>=5/ >=4/ positive).
These are display thresholds chosen for this learning site, not an official
wordfreq classification, proficiency grade or census of native speakers.
The source score describes a word form, not its part of speech or a specific
meaning. Numbers reflect this fixed data version, not a live 2026 frequency.

Phrases: exact contiguous token matches in the 10,000 top English sentences
from orgtre/top-open-subtitles-sentences, derived from OPUS OpenSubtitles2018.
https://github.com/orgtre/top-open-subtitles-sentences
https://opus.nlpl.eu/OpenSubtitles-v2018.php
Lison, P. and Tiedemann, J. (2016), OpenSubtitles2016: Extracting Large Parallel
Corpora from Movie and TV Subtitles. Proceedings of LREC 2016.
The upstream project documents the lists under the terms of the underlying
corpus and its processing code under CC BY 3.0. Raw subtitles are not included
in this site. Only aggregate match counts for curriculum expressions are kept.

Phrase partial-star fill uses log10(weighted matches per million)+3 as
a sample-only Zipf-like input to the same visual mapping. Coarse source
tiers use weighted occurrences per million words IN THIS TOP-SENTENCE
SAMPLE: >=100 =>three; >=10 =>two; any recorded match below10 =>one. These
figures are sample-specific, biased toward common short subtitle sentences,
not whole-corpus conversation estimates. The corpus can contain translated
or scripted dialogue. Word and phrase tiers use different sources and should
not be directly compared. Unmatched phrases show unmeasured, never low-frequency.
No phrase score is inferred by combining frequencies of its component words.

Metadata and source file SHA256 are in frequency-reference-data.js. The full
underlying datasets are not downloaded by a visitor. Stars do not add tasks,
change five-sentence goals or count as evidence of learning/mastery.
